## Why I Took It

**You can't secure a network you don't understand.** Before going deeper into security, I wanted a solid foundation in how networks actually work. I wanted to understand how traffic moves, how devices talk to each other, and where things break.


## How I Prepared

- **Professor Messer's free video course** This gave me the information on every objective encompassed by the exam.
- **Practice exams** Jason Dion has exams I utilized to find gaps in my knowledge and focus on my weaknesses.

## The Exam

The exam is very similar to the Security+ exam. It has up to 90 questions with a mix of multiple choice and performance-based questions. The PBQs show up first and can eat your time, so I prioritized the multiple-choice section of the exam. Overall difficulty was about what I expected as the layout and wording are very similar to the Security+ exam.


## Key Takeaways

**1. Network troubleshooting methodology**
Gave me a structured approach to troubleshooting: identify the problem, form a theory, test it, and work through the OSI model layer by layer.

**2. Firewalls and network segmentation**
Understanding ports, protocols, and how traffic flows between network segments is crucial for developing skills to enhance security posture.

**3. Ports and protocols**
Knowing what runs on which port sounds basic, but it's also the first thing I check when investigating suspicious traffic, since an unexpected service on an open port is often the first clue something is wrong.


## Tips for Others

- **Master subnetting early.** It shows up throughout the exam, and practicing it until it's second nature saves a lot of time.
- **Learn the OSI model inside and out.** Many questions come down to knowing which layer a device, protocol, or problem belongs to.
- **Memorize common ports and protocols.** Flashcards work well here.

## Was It Worth It?

**Yes.** Network+ is the foundation everything in security builds on. Firewalls, intrusion detection, traffic analysis, and incident response all assume you understand networking. I'd recommend it to anyone starting out in IT, and especially to anyone planning to move into security.
