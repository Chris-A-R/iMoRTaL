# CompTIA Security+

**Issuer:** CompTIA

| | |
|---|---|
| **Earned:** | 10/2025 |
| **Exam:** | SY0-701 |
| **Exam Format:** | Up to 90 questions, 90 minutes, 750 to pass (scale 100–900) |
| **Verify:** | [View badge on Credly](https://www.credly.com/badges/c7e22a43-71ad-4cdb-8af5-77b7a1de2f04/public_url) |
{: .cert-facts}

## Why I Took It

Security+ was my first step into security. I wanted a way to prove I understood the fundamentals. It's the baseline cert most security roles ask for, and it gave me the vocabulary and framework to move from general IT into a security role.


## How I Prepared

- **Professor Messer's video course** This gave me the information on every objective encompassed by the exam.
- **Practice Exams** Jason Dion has practice exams on Udemy that I used to gain insight on how the exam would be worded.

## The Exam

The exam has up to 90 questions in 90 minutes. The PBQs show up first; I flagged them and came back after the multiple choice. Overall difficulty was a bit harder than the practice exams. The exam questions were sometimes worded in a way that would ask for the best answer when multiple correct answers are displayed.


## Key Takeaways

**1. Identity and access management**
Concepts like least privilege, MFA, and role-based access heavily influenced how I managed identity in Microsoft Entra.

**2. Defense in depth**
Security+ taught me that no single control is enough. That mindset shaped how I approached securing an organization: firewalls at the perimeter, patch management on endpoints, and user training to round it out.

**3. Incident response fundamentals**
The incident response lifecycle gave me a structured way to handle security events instead of reacting on the fly.


## Tips for Others

- **Use the exam objectives as your checklist.** CompTIA publishes them for free, and everything on the exam comes from them. Use this as your reference to track your studying progress.
- **Take practice exams.** They show you where your knowledge gaps are and train you for CompTIA's wording.
- **Learn the "why," not just the "what".** Questions often describe a scenario and ask what you'd do, so memorizing definitions alone won't be beneficial.


## Was It Worth It?


**Yes.** Security+ won't make you an expert, but it can be the foundation everything else builds on. I'd recommend it to anyone looking to move into security from IT or another field.
