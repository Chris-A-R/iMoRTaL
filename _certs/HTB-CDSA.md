# HTB Certified Defensive Security Analyst

## Why I Took It

I pursued the HTB CDSA because I wanted hands-on SOC skills, not just theory. Unlike multiple-choice exams, this certification requires investigating real attack scenarios with tools like Splunk and Elastic. I also experienced writing a professional incident report!

## How I Prepared

I completed HTB Academy’s SOC Analyst job-role path, which is required before you can take the exam. I focused on understanding each technique rather than just following steps. I saved every Splunk and Elastic query I thought would be useful. I noted what tools were going to be useful given different situations.

## The Exam

The CDSA gives you 7 days of access to a lab environment where you investigate real-world attack scenarios and submit a professional incident report including all scenarios. HTB provides a report template, and everything runs in the browser through Pwnbox, so there was no setup to worry about.

**Pacing:** I spent roughly 4 days total investigating all scenarios and about 2 days writing the professional report. The last day was utilized to ensure the professional report was to my satisfaction. Seven days sounds like a lot, but the investigation and the report both take a good portion of time to complete thoroughly. I'd recommend documenting findings with timestamps and screenshots the moment you find them instead of saving the write-up for the end.

**Difficulty:** Harder than I expected. The SOC Analyst path covers everything you need, but the exam doesn't walk you through anything. You have to know what to look for and how to connect your findings.

**What surprised me:** How it was treated like a real investigation rather than a test. Without the time I spent learning to write queries, the investigation would have taken much longer.

## Key Takeaways

**1. Log Analysis**
The biggest takeaway from the CDSA was learning to start from one suspicious event and follow the trail.

**2. Attacker Mindset**
Studying how attackers establish themselves after gaining access. Utilizing persistence, privilege escalation, and lateral movement to dig deeper into the environment.

**3. Incident Documentation**
The report requirement made me stay disciplined about documentation. Recording findings with timestamps and evidence as I investigate.

## Tips for Others

**Learn your query languages . . . seriously.** Get comfortable with Splunk and Elastic's query syntax while working through the SOC Analyst path. This will mark the difference between finding something in minutes and losing hours scrolling through events manually.

## Was It Worth It?

**It was worth it.** The CDSA is one of the few certifications that actually tests whether you can do the job. The SOC Analyst path taught me practical skills and the exam forced me to apply them in the way a real investigation would.
